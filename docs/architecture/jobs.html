Events, jobs and the scheduler

An in-memory event bus tells the rest of core what happened. A durable job queue does the work, and survives a crash. A scheduler puts recurring work on that queue.

1

The three pieces

Piece Lives in Survives a restart? Job
Event bus memory no tell listeners that something happened
Job queue raw.db yes run work at least once, with retries
Scheduler raw.db yes enqueue recurring work on time, once

Nothing here calls a model or a mail provider. Ingest, sync and enrichment arrive in later prompts as job handlers; this layer only runs them.

2

The job lifecycle

A job is a row in raw.db. Workers take jobs by claiming them: one short transaction marks the best runnable job as running and gives the worker a lease. A worker that dies simply stops renewing its lease; once the lease expires, the job is queued again.

sequenceDiagram
    participant P as Producer
    participant Q as Queue (raw.db)
    participant W as Worker
    participant H as Handler
    P->>Q: enqueue(kind, payload, dedupe_key)
    Q-->>P: job id (or the existing one)
    W->>Q: claim(kinds, limits)
    Note over Q: reclaim expired leases, skip kinds at their limit,<br/>pick highest priority, set lease, attempts + 1
    Q-->>W: job
    W->>H: handler(payload, job)
    loop while it runs
        W->>Q: heartbeat (extends the lease)
    end
    alt success
        H-->>W: returns
        W->>Q: complete, status succeeded
    else error and attempts left
        H-->>W: raises
        W->>Q: fail, queued again after backoff
        Note over Q: delay doubles each attempt, capped, with jitter
    else permanent error or out of attempts
        W->>Q: fail, status failed (last_error kept)
    end

States are queued, running, succeeded, failed and cancelled. Every change is also published as a JobStateChanged event.

3

Guarantees and rules

  • At least once. If a worker dies mid-job, the job runs again after its lease expires, so handlers must be idempotent. A worker that lost its lease cannot mark the job done: the finish is fenced by the worker's id.
  • Dedupe. An enqueue with a dedupe_key that is already queued or running does nothing and returns the existing job. Once the job finishes the key is free again.
  • Priority. Higher runs first: user-initiated (100), ingest (50), backfill (10). Within a priority, the job that became runnable first goes first.
  • Retries. After the Nth failed attempt a job waits min(backoff_max, backoff_base * 2^(N-1)) seconds, shortened by up to backoff_jitter at random. After max_attempts it is failed. A handler can raise PermanentJobError to fail at once, and a payload that does not validate fails at once too.
  • Errors are stored safely. last_error has secrets redacted and is cut to 500 characters.
  • Per-kind concurrency. A kind can be capped, for example one embed job at a time for a single local GPU. Handlers declare a default; [jobs] kind_limits in config.toml wins. The cap is enforced in the claim itself, so it holds across all workers.
  • Cancellation. A queued job is cancelled immediately. A running job is interrupted (its handler task is cancelled), so handlers should be cancel-safe.
  • Shutdown. stop() lets in-flight jobs finish for a grace period, then cancels them and releases their leases without counting the attempt.
  • Long jobs. A running job renews its lease every third of the lease length, so a slow local model can take as long as it needs.
4

Events

The bus carries typed, frozen events: sync progress, message stored, parsed, indexed, embedded, enriched, read-state changes, job state changes and plugin health. There are two kinds of listener:

  • Handlers are internal components. Every matching event reaches every handler, none is ever dropped, and a handler that raises is logged and skipped without affecting anyone else.
  • Streams feed clients (the server-sent events endpoint later). Each has a bounded queue and, if a consumer falls behind, the oldest events are dropped and counted. A slow stream never blocks the publisher.
5

Results, parents and actors

A job can store a result, which is checked against a schema for its kind before it is saved. It can name a parent job, for splitting work into pieces, and it records its actor (user, agent or system). Besides core's own workers, external agents can lease jobs too: see the swarm.

6

The scheduler

A schedule is either a fixed interval or a five-field cron expression, evaluated in UTC (no daylight-saving surprises). Its next_run is stored in raw.db. A tick enqueues the due jobs and advances next_run in one transaction, so a restart neither repeats a run nor skips one. If the process was down for several periods, the missed runs collapse into a single run.

The cron parser is about a hundred lines with no dependency. It covers *, lists, ranges, steps and month and weekday names, which is everything "sync every five minutes" or "nightly backfill" needs; a full library would add a dependency and time-zone handling that nothing here uses.

7

Testing

Everything above is tested with a fake clock and no sleeping: advance the clock, call drain() on the runner or tick() on the scheduler, and look at the database. The tests live in src/oaken/core/tests/.
